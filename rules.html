Ahkam of travel

The rulings

On the prayers of a traveller, according to Sayyid Ali al-Husayni al-Sistani.

Every rule below carries the mas'ala it comes from, in Tawḍīḥ al-Masā'il Jāmiʿ, vol. 1. Nothing is stated here that is not in that text.

The distance

  • The journey must be not less than 8 farsakh, about 44 km. 1695
  • Under that — even slightly — the prayer is full, and one is not obliged to investigate the distance, though investigating is a recommended precaution. There is no margin either side of the line. 1698
  • Outward and return are added together, and neither leg need reach 4 farsakh on its own: three out and five back is a journey. 1696
  • That holds even if the return falls on a later day; praying full as well is a recommended precaution, not an obligation. 1697
  • Repeated trips between two places less than 4 farsakh apart never add up, however great the total mileage. 1702
  • The road taken decides it: where one route is under 8 farsakh and another over, the long way is shortened and the short way is not. 1703

Where the measuring starts and ends

  • It starts where one is counted a traveller — the end of the city or village, not the front door. 1704
  • It ends at the destination itself within the far town, not at that town's edge. 1705
  • The text's own example: a home 15 km from Mashhad, to a place in Mashhad 25 km away and back, is a journey (50 km); to a place 20 km away and back is not (40 km). 1705
  • Tehran is not reckoned among the great cities for this purpose, so the measuring runs from the end of the whole city. 1704 fn.2

The intention

  • The distance must be intended at the outset. Deciding to go further only after arriving does not join the two together — though from that point a fresh journey may qualify. 1706
  • One who does not know how far he will go prays full; the return may still qualify on its own. 1707
  • A journey conditional on something uncertain does not qualify unless he is confident of it. 1708

What stops the legs being added together

  • Intending ten days' residence at the destination, or stopping in a waṭan of one's own on the way, ends the journey there. Each leg must then reach 8 farsakh on its own. 1719
  • So: 4 farsakh out with a ten-day stay is full both ways; 8 farsakh out with a ten-day stay is shortened on the road and full only at the stay; 7 out and 9 back is full going and shortened returning. 1719 fn.2
  • The mere possibility of such a stop is enough to make the prayer full. 1720
  • Abandoning that intention does not restore the earlier ruling; the reckoning starts again from where one stands. 1721

Ten days in one place

  • The intention must be firm. Doubt, or mere supposition, means one prays shortened — even if he does in fact stay ten days. 1782
  • A rationally significant possibility of being called away has the same effect, even if it never arises. 1785
  • The ten days must be in one named settlement. Seven days in Najaf and three in Kūfa is not ten; nor Tehran and Karaj; nor two neighbouring villages, even within sight of one another. 1789
  • But districts of one city do combine, however far apart — the measure is the name, never the distance. 1790
  • One who has formed the intention prays full, must keep the obligatory fast, and prays the nawāfil of ẓuhr, ʿaṣr and ʿishāʾ. 1808

Where the shortening begins

  • Leaving one's waṭan, at the ḥadd al-tarakhkhuṣ: where the people of the town — and of its dependencies — can no longer see him, the sign being that he cannot see them. 1755, 1756
  • Leaving anywhere else, as soon as he leaves the town: outside the waṭan the ḥadd al-tarakhkhuṣ has no bearing at all. 1755
  • Returning, he shortens until he actually enters his waṭan; and heading for a ten-day stay, until he reaches it. 1757
  • While he doubts whether he has reached it, he prays full. 1761

Who is not a traveller at all

  • One travelling for an unlawful purpose, or whose travel is itself unlawful. 1722
  • One whose dwelling travels with him. 1736
  • One whose work is travel — a driver, a pilot — from the very first journey, even if the work is temporary. 1739, 1743
  • One who travels often: ten or more travel days a month is full; eight or nine is an obligatory precaution to pray both; seven or fewer is shortened. 1741
  • A ten-day stay does not reset this: he still prays full on the first journey afterwards. Many summaries say otherwise; that is another marjaʿ's position. 1750
  • Travelling to hunt for sport is full going out and shortened coming back. Travel for recreation or sightseeing is shortened. A journey custom reckons futile is prayed both. 1732, 1734, 1735

What this calculator will not decide

Several of the rulings turn on custom, and the text defers to it rather than giving a number. Whether somewhere is your waṭan; whether you are one who travels often; whether a journey is futile; whether two stays are in one settlement or two; where exactly the ḥadd al-tarakhkhuṣ falls. The calculator asks you these and stops if unanswered. It does not infer them. 1739, 1764, 1789

Source: Tawḍīḥ al-Masā'il Jāmiʿ, vol. 1, masāʾil 1695–1790 and 1803–1808, at sistani.org. Three things commonly stated elsewhere are deliberately absent here because they are not in that range: the four places of choice, the thirty days of hesitation, and the traveller's exemption from fasting. Each needs its own citation before it is stated. This is a tool for measuring and applying the common cases; it is not a substitute for a qualified scholar.
