            3 The conditions Standard journey — open to check

            These are set for an ordinary journey. Each is a judgement of custom rather than of measurement, so change any that does not describe yours.

            Questions about the journey
            I intended the whole distance when I set out.Deciding to go further after arriving starts a fresh journey. 1706
            The destination is one of my homelands (waṭan).Birthplace, a place chosen for life, or one made your base for about eighteen months or more. 1764
            I will stay ten continuous days at the destination.This does not make the journey full — it stops the two legs being added together. 1719

            Doubt or supposition means you pray shortened, even if you do in fact stay ten days. 1782, 1785

            All ten days in one named settlement.Seven in Najaf and three in Kūfa does not count as ten — nor two neighbouring villages, however close. Districts of one city do count, however far apart. 1789, 1790
            I might stop in a homeland of mine, or decide on a ten-day stay, before the distance is complete.The possibility is enough on its own. 1720
            Travel is my work, or I travel very often.A driver or pilot; or ten or more travel days a month. Such a person prays full at any distance. Borderline cases belong with a scholar. 1739, 1741
            The purpose of the journey is unlawful, or the journey is customarily futile.An unlawful purpose is prayed full; a journey with no rational purpose is prayed both. A holiday is neither. 1722, 1735
